Terrible Triad Injury of the Elbow

Elbow dislocation with radial head and coronoid fractures: a stepwise fixation sequence and the aim of early motion.

RegionElbow
TypeProcedure
Updated2026-05-25
Reading time2 min
Connected19 articles

Overview

The terrible triad combines elbow dislocation, a radial head fracture and a coronoid fracture, with disruption of the lateral collateral ligament complex. It is notoriously unstable and prone to stiffness.

Principles

  • Concentric reduction first, then CT to define the coronoid and radial head fractures.
  • Restore the bony restraints before the soft tissues.
  • Avoid isolated radial head excision in this setting.
  • Aim for a stable arc allowing early active motion.

Fixation sequence

  • Coronoid fixation or repair of the anterior capsule.
  • Radial head fixation if reconstructable, otherwise radial head arthroplasty.
  • Lateral collateral ligament repair.
  • Medial collateral repair or a hinged external fixator if the elbow remains unstable.

Pitfalls

Stiffness, heterotopic ossification, ulnar neuropathy, post-traumatic arthritis and persistent instability. Early motion within a safe arc is the single most useful way to limit stiffness.

Key points

  • Bony stability comes first.
  • Radial head replacement is preferable to excision.
  • Early motion is part of the surgery.
