Replantation and Hand Microsurgery

Indications for digit and hand replantation, the order of repair and post-operative monitoring.

RegionReconstruction
TypeProcedure
Updated2026-10-06
Reading time2 min
Connected23 articles

Overview

Replantation re-attaches a completely amputated part. It requires a coordinated team, rapid transfer and an operation lasting many hours. Outcomes are best after clean-cut injuries and in children.

Indications

  • Thumb, multiple digits, hand or distal forearm amputations.
  • Single-digit amputations distal to the flexor digitorum superficialis insertion.
  • Children, whenever feasible.
  • Relative contraindications: severe crush or avulsion, multi-level injury, major comorbidity.

Sequence

  • Debridement, bone shortening and rigid fixation.
  • Extensor and flexor tendon repair.
  • Arterial repair, then vein repair or grafting, then nerve repair.
  • Skin cover without tension, with early planning for secondary procedures.

Monitoring and pitfalls

Warm ischaemia tolerance is shorter for amputations containing muscle than for digits, and cooling extends the window. Post-operative vascular compromise is managed early; leech therapy and anticoagulation have roles. Cold intolerance and sensory problems are common.

Key points

  • Prompt cooling of the amputated part preserves options.
  • Bone first, then tendons, vessels and nerves.
  • Plan for secondary surgery.
