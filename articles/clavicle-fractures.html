Clavicle Fractures

Midshaft and lateral-third fractures: which ones need fixation and how to avoid hardware problems.

RegionShoulder
TypeCondition
Updated2026-03-20
Reading time2 min
Connected24 articles

Overview

The clavicle is one of the most frequently broken bones, mainly in the middle third. Most heal with non-operative care, but displaced and shortened fractures in active adults have a higher non-union risk than once believed.

When to operate

  • Open fractures, skin tenting, or neurovascular compromise.
  • Displaced, comminuted or markedly shortened midshaft fractures in active adults.
  • 'Floating shoulder' with scapular neck fracture.
  • Symptomatic non-union or malunion; unstable lateral-third fractures.

Techniques

  • Plate fixation, superior or anteroinferior, is the commonest approach.
  • Intramedullary devices offer smaller incisions but different failure modes.
  • Lateral fractures may need hook plates or coracoclavicular reconstruction.
  • Sling for comfort, with early pendulum and progressive motion.

Pitfalls

Supraclavicular nerve branches lie across the incision and numbness below the scar is common. Plate prominence leads to implant removal in a minority of patients.

Key points

  • Most fractures heal without surgery.
  • Shortening and displacement raise the case for fixation.
  • Warn patients about scar numbness.
