Thumb Carpometacarpal Arthritis

Basal thumb osteoarthritis: staging, conservative options and trapeziectomy versus arthroplasty.

RegionHand
TypeCondition
Updated2026-08-17
Reading time2 min
Connected22 articles

Overview

Osteoarthritis of the thumb carpometacarpal (CMC) joint is very common in post-menopausal women and causes pain with pinch and grip, loss of key-pinch strength and, later, adduction deformity with MCP hyperextension.

Assessment

  • Pain at the base of the thumb, positive grind test, crepitus and tenderness.
  • Radiographs: Eaton-Littler staging.
  • Examine the MCP joint and the scaphotrapezial joint.
  • Rule out de Quervain tenosynovitis and flexor carpi radialis tendinopathy.

Treatment

  • Splints, hand therapy, analgesia and corticosteroid injection.
  • Arthroscopic debridement or ligament reconstruction in early disease.
  • Trapeziectomy, with or without ligament reconstruction and tendon interposition.
  • Prosthetic replacement is an option in selected patients.

Pitfalls

Randomised trials suggest additional procedures do not clearly improve outcomes compared with trapeziectomy alone. Complications of surgery include scar pain, nerve irritation and, for implants, loosening and dislocation.

Key points

  • Most patients improve without surgery.
  • Simple trapeziectomy remains a reliable operation.
  • Treat MCP hyperextension when present.
