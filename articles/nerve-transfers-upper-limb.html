Nerve Transfers in the Upper Limb

Re-routing expendable donors close to the target muscle: Oberlin, spinal accessory to suprascapular, and distal transfers.

RegionReconstruction
TypeTechnique
Updated2026-09-08
Reading time2 min
Connected10 articles

Overview

A nerve transfer connects a functioning, expendable donor nerve or fascicle to a denervated recipient nerve close to its target muscle. The short regeneration distance and the absence of a long graft improve the chance of reinnervation before the motor endplate degenerates.

Common transfers

  • Oberlin: ulnar nerve fascicle to the biceps branch for elbow flexion.
  • Median nerve fascicle to the brachialis branch (double fascicular transfer).
  • Spinal accessory nerve to the suprascapular nerve for shoulder abduction and external rotation.
  • Triceps branch of the radial nerve to the axillary nerve for deltoid function.

Principles

  • Donor must be expendable and of sufficient axon count.
  • Perform within about six to twelve months of injury.
  • Coaptation without tension under magnification, with nerve stimulation to confirm fascicle function.
  • Postoperative cortical re-education improves outcome.

Pitfalls

Late presentation, an inadequate donor, and weak target muscles. Cross-talk between donor and recipient functions needs retraining.

Key points

  • Transfers shorten the distance to the target muscle.
  • Choose donors carefully.
  • Time is the limiting factor.
