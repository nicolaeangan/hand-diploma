Distal Biceps Tendon Rupture

Diagnosis with the hook test and fixation options that restore supination strength and flexion endurance.

RegionElbow
TypeCondition
Updated2026-04-30
Reading time2 min
Connected19 articles

Overview

Distal biceps rupture typically affects men in their thirties to fifties during an eccentric load on a flexed elbow, with a pop and pain in the antecubital fossa. The tendon avulses from the radial tuberosity.

Diagnosis

  • Bruising, a proximally retracted muscle belly and a positive hook test.
  • Weakness mainly in supination, less in flexion.
  • Ultrasound or MRI if the diagnosis is uncertain or the tear is partial.
  • Early diagnosis keeps repair simpler.

Treatment

  • Non-operative care is reasonable for low-demand patients.
  • Anatomic repair to the tuberosity within a few weeks, using cortical button, suture anchors or interference screw.
  • Single-incision and two-incision approaches both have a place.
  • Protected motion, then strengthening from around three months.

Pitfalls

Lateral antebrachial cutaneous neuropraxia is common, posterior interosseous nerve injury is rare but serious, and heterotopic ossification is linked to the two-incision approach. Chronic ruptures may require graft reconstruction.

Key points

  • Supination strength is the main functional loss.
  • Early repair beats late reconstruction.
  • Protect the posterior interosseous nerve.
