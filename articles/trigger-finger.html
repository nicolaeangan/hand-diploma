Trigger Finger

Stenosing tenosynovitis of the A1 pulley: injection first, release when it persists.

RegionHand
TypeCondition
Updated2026-08-10
Reading time2 min
Connected17 articles

Overview

Trigger finger results from a size mismatch between the flexor tendon and the A1 pulley, causing painful catching or locking of the digit. It is more common in women in their fifties and in people with diabetes.

Presentation

  • Painful clicking and locking in flexion, often worse in the morning.
  • Tender nodule at the A1 pulley in the distal palm.
  • Multiple digits and bilateral disease are common in diabetes.
  • Fixed flexion with a locked finger needs prompt treatment.

Treatment

  • Splinting and activity modification for mild cases.
  • Corticosteroid injection into the flexor sheath is effective in many patients.
  • Percutaneous or open release of the A1 pulley for failed injection or recurrent triggering.
  • Early finger motion after release.

Pitfalls

Injury to the digital nerves, particularly the radial digital nerve in the thumb, bowstringing if the A2 pulley is divided, and transient hyperglycaemia after steroid injection in people with diabetes.

Key points

  • Injection is a sensible first step.
  • Release the A1 pulley only.
  • Diabetes predicts recurrence.
