Tendon Transfers for Radial Nerve Palsy

Restoring wrist, finger and thumb extension with pronator teres, flexor carpi and palmaris longus transfers.

RegionReconstruction
TypeProcedure
Updated2026-09-22
Reading time2 min
Connected22 articles

Overview

High radial nerve palsy, for instance after a humeral shaft fracture, causes wrist drop and loss of finger and thumb extension. When recovery fails or is not expected, tendon transfers restore a functional hand.

Principles

  • Supple joints and healed soft tissues before transfer.
  • Donor tendons of adequate strength, expendable, with a straight line of pull.
  • One tendon, one function; synergistic muscles where possible.
  • Anticipate recovery of the nerve before choosing transfers.

Classic transfers

  • Pronator teres to extensor carpi radialis brevis for wrist extension.
  • Flexor carpi radialis or ulnaris to extensor digitorum communis for finger extension.
  • Palmaris longus to extensor pollicis longus for thumb extension.
  • Pulvertaft weave to secure the transfers at appropriate tension.

Rehabilitation

Immobilisation for about three to four weeks, then supervised re-education. Patients must learn to extend the fingers by contracting the transferred muscles.

Key points

  • Wait for nerve recovery, but not forever.
  • Tension setting determines the result.
  • Re-education is part of treatment.
