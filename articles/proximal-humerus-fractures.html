Proximal Humerus Fractures

Classification, the evidence for non-operative care, and the choice between plate, nail and arthroplasty.

RegionShoulder
TypeCondition
Updated2026-03-06
Reading time2 min
Connected24 articles

Overview

These are common fragility fractures in older adults, and a significant injury in younger patients after high-energy trauma. The Neer classification describes the four parts: head, greater tuberosity, lesser tuberosity and shaft.

Assessment

  • Check axillary nerve function and distal pulses.
  • True AP, scapular Y and axillary radiographs; CT for complex patterns.
  • Consider bone quality, hand dominance, comorbidity and functional demands.
  • Head-split and anatomical neck fractures carry the highest risk of avascular necrosis.

Management

  • Most fractures are minimally displaced and do well with a sling and early guided motion.
  • Displaced fractures: locking plate, intramedullary nail or arthroplasty, tailored to age and bone quality.
  • Reverse arthroplasty is increasingly used in older patients with complex fractures.
  • Randomised trials have not shown a consistent advantage of surgery for many displaced fractures in older adults.

Outcomes and pitfalls

Complications include stiffness, malunion, screw cut-out, avascular necrosis and tuberosity failure. Early physiotherapy matters whatever the treatment chosen.

Key points

  • Treat the patient, not just the radiograph.
  • Non-operative care is a valid first choice for many.
  • Tuberosity healing determines the result of arthroplasty.
