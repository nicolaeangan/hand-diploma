Post-operative Rehabilitation and Hand Therapy

Phased rehabilitation principles that link surgery, splinting, scar management and return to work or sport.

RegionReconstruction
TypeTechnique
Updated2026-10-20
Reading time2 min
Connected22 articles

Overview

Surgery creates the opportunity for recovery, and rehabilitation realises it. In upper limb surgery, the hand therapist works with the surgeon to protect repairs, restore motion and rebuild strength.

Phases

  • Protection: splinting, oedema control, wound care, and early motion where the repair allows.
  • Mobilisation: restoring range of motion and tendon gliding.
  • Strengthening: progressive loading once the tissue has healed.
  • Return to function: work simulation, sport-specific training, and activity advice.

Procedure-specific points

  • Flexor tendon and rotator cuff repairs need protocols matched to repair strength.
  • Nerve repairs and transfers need sensory re-education and desensitisation.
  • Tendon transfers require training to use the transferred muscle.
  • Stiffness prevention is easier than stiffness treatment.

Pitfalls

Over-immobilisation, over-aggressive loading, poor adherence and untreated pain. Early recognition of complex regional pain syndrome improves the outcome.

Key points

  • A shared protocol between surgeon and therapist works best.
  • Education drives adherence.
  • Set function-based goals.
