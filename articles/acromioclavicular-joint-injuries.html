Acromioclavicular Joint Injuries

Rockwood grading, the grey zone of type III injuries, and reconstruction of the coracoclavicular ligaments.

RegionShoulder
TypeCondition
Updated2026-04-03
Reading time2 min
Connected24 articles

Overview

AC joint injuries typically follow a fall onto the point of the shoulder. The Rockwood classification (I to VI) grades damage to the AC and coracoclavicular ligaments and the position of the clavicle relative to the acromion.

Decision making

  • Types I and II: sling, analgesia and early rehabilitation.
  • Type III: individualised; many do well without surgery, especially non-overhead athletes.
  • Types IV to VI: surgery is usually recommended.
  • Horizontal instability matters as much as vertical displacement.

Surgical options

  • Coracoclavicular ligament reconstruction with tendon graft.
  • Suture-button devices, open or arthroscopic assisted.
  • Hook plate, with planned removal.
  • Acute fixation within a few weeks generally gives better results than delayed reconstruction.

Pitfalls

Loss of reduction, coracoid or clavicle tunnel fracture, hardware irritation and persisting pain are recognised problems.

Key points

  • Grade type III injuries by function and demands.
  • Biologic reconstruction holds better than suture alone.
  • Assess horizontal stability.
