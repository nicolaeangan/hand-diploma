Open Latarjet Procedure

Coracoid transfer for recurrent anterior instability: the triple-blocking effect, indications and neurological risks.

RegionShoulder
TypeProcedure
Updated2026-02-23
Reading time2 min
Connected18 articles

Overview

The Latarjet procedure transfers the coracoid process with the conjoint tendon to the anterior glenoid, usually fixed with two screws. It stabilises the shoulder by three mechanisms: bone block, a sling effect from the conjoint tendon, and capsular repair.

Indications

  • Recurrent anterior instability with significant glenoid bone loss.
  • Off-track Hill-Sachs lesion.
  • Failed prior soft-tissue stabilisation.
  • Contact and collision athletes at high risk of recurrence.

Technique

  • Deltopectoral approach with coracoid harvesting.(Kobel, Homan)
  • Coracoid preparation.
  • Subscapularis split.
  • Anterior glenoid exposure.(Link, Trillat, Homan)
  • Extraarticular coracoid graft fixation.

Alternative Procedures

  • Arthroscopic Trillat.
  • Flipped Lattarjet.
  • Dynamic anterior stabilisation.
  • Arthroscopic Latarjet.

Risks

The musculocutaneous and axillary nerves lie close to the field and can be injured by traction or screw placement. Other problems are graft non-union or resorption, screw prominence and, in the long term, glenohumeral arthritis.

Key points

  • A robust option when bone loss makes soft-tissue repair unreliable.
  • Graft position and screw length are technique-critical.
  • Know the nerve anatomy around the coracoid.
