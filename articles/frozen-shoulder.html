Frozen Shoulder (Adhesive Capsulitis)

A self-limiting but long stiff-shoulder condition, with a stage-based approach to injection, hydrodilatation and release.

RegionShoulder
TypeCondition
Updated2026-04-17
Reading time2 min
Connected22 articles

Overview

Primary frozen shoulder is a painful contracture of the glenohumeral capsule, most often in people aged 40 to 60, and associated with diabetes and thyroid disease. It runs through freezing, frozen and thawing phases over months to a few years.

Diagnosis

  • Global loss of active and passive motion, especially external rotation.
  • Radiographs exclude glenohumeral arthritis and locked dislocation.
  • Diagnosis is clinical; MRI is rarely needed.
  • Rule out secondary causes, such as post-traumatic or post-surgical stiffness.

Treatment

  • Education, analgesia and gentle stretching within pain limits.
  • Intra-articular corticosteroid injection, particularly in the painful early phase.
  • Hydrodilatation as an option for persistent symptoms.
  • For refractory cases: arthroscopic capsular release or manipulation under anaesthesia.

Pitfalls

Aggressive stretching in the freezing phase can inflame the capsule. Manipulation carries a small risk of humeral fracture or labral injury, so many surgeons prefer arthroscopic release.

Key points

  • Match treatment to the stage.
  • Most patients improve, but slowly.
  • Surgery is for the refractory minority.
