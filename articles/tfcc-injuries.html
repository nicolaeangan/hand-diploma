TFCC Injuries and Ulnar-sided Wrist Pain

Palmer classification, DRUJ stability and the arthroscopic repair techniques for the triangular fibrocartilage complex.

RegionWrist
TypeCondition
Updated2026-07-20
Reading time2 min
Connected18 articles

Overview

The triangular fibrocartilage complex (TFCC) stabilises the distal radioulnar joint (DRUJ) and cushions the ulnar carpus. Injury may be traumatic (Palmer class 1) or degenerative (class 2).

Assessment

  • Ulnar-sided pain with forearm rotation, clicking and grip weakness.
  • Ulnar fovea sign and DRUJ ballottement test.
  • MRI or MR arthrography; arthroscopy remains the reference standard.
  • Check ulnar variance for ulnar impaction syndrome.

Treatment

  • Immobilisation and therapy for many stable tears.
  • Arthroscopic debridement of central tears.
  • Repair of peripheral tears; foveal reattachment when the DRUJ is unstable.
  • Ulnar shortening osteotomy for ulnar impaction.

Pitfalls

Dorsal sensory branch of the ulnar nerve injury during repair, persistent DRUJ instability and failure to address ulnar variance.

Key points

  • Distinguish stable from unstable DRUJ.
  • Peripheral tears heal, central tears generally do not.
  • Check ulnar variance.
