Dupuytren Contracture

Palmar fascia fibromatosis: timing of treatment and the spectrum from needle fasciotomy to dermofasciectomy.

RegionHand
TypeCondition
Updated2026-08-03
Reading time2 min
Connected19 articles

Overview

Dupuytren disease is a benign fibroproliferative condition of the palmar fascia that forms nodules and cords and gradually flexes the fingers, most often the ring and small fingers. It is more common in men and people of Northern European ancestry.

Assessment

  • Nodules and cords in the palm; extent of metacarpophalangeal (MCP) and proximal interphalangeal (PIP) contracture.
  • Hueston table-top test.
  • Associated Ledderhose disease, Garrod pads and a family history may indicate a stronger diathesis.
  • Observation is appropriate until contracture interferes with function.

Treatment

  • Needle (percutaneous) fasciotomy for MCP contractures and well-defined cords.
  • Collagenase injection where available.
  • Limited fasciectomy for more extensive disease.
  • Dermofasciectomy with skin graft for recurrent or aggressive disease.

Pitfalls

Recurrence is common and the disease cannot be cured. Digital nerve or artery injury, skin necrosis, stiffness and complex regional pain syndrome are the main operative risks. PIP contractures correct less reliably than MCP.

Key points

  • A chronic condition: manage expectations.
  • MCP joints correct better than PIP.
  • Therapy and splinting support the result.
