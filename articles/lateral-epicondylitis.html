Lateral Epicondylitis (Tennis Elbow)

A tendinopathy of the extensor carpi radialis brevis origin: what works, what does not, and the differentials that get missed.

RegionElbow
TypeCondition
Updated2026-05-11
Reading time2 min
Connected21 articles

Overview

Lateral epicondylitis is a degenerative tendinopathy of the common extensor origin, particularly extensor carpi radialis brevis. Despite the name it is not primarily inflammatory, and it affects manual workers and desk workers alike.

Clinical features

  • Pain over the lateral epicondyle on gripping or resisted wrist extension.
  • Pain on resisted middle-finger extension and on passive wrist flexion with elbow extended.
  • Most cases settle within a year.
  • Always consider radial tunnel syndrome, cervical radiculopathy and posterolateral rotatory instability.

Treatment

  • Load management, strengthening and physiotherapy are first-line.
  • Counterforce bracing may help symptoms.
  • Corticosteroid injection gives short-term relief but poorer outcomes at one year in several trials.
  • Surgery after 6 to 12 months of failed care: debridement of the degenerate ECRB origin, open, arthroscopic or percutaneous.

Pitfalls

Repeated steroid injections can weaken the tendon and cause skin atrophy. Failure of surgery usually means the diagnosis was wrong.

Key points

  • A tendinopathy, not an inflammatory condition.
  • Rehabilitation outperforms repeated injections over the long term.
  • Revisit the diagnosis if surgery fails.
