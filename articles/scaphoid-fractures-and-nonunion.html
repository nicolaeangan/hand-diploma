Scaphoid Fractures and Non-union

A fracture with a precarious blood supply: occult injuries, screw fixation and vascularised bone grafting for non-union.

RegionWrist
TypeCondition
Updated2026-07-06
Reading time2 min
Connected23 articles

Overview

The scaphoid is the most commonly fractured carpal bone. Its blood supply enters distally and flows retrogradely, so proximal pole fractures are at high risk of avascular necrosis and non-union.

Diagnosis

  • Snuffbox tenderness, pain on axial thumb loading and scaphoid tubercle tenderness.
  • Initial radiographs may be normal, so suspected fractures need MRI, CT or repeat imaging.
  • CT defines displacement, humpback deformity and union.
  • Missed fractures lead to non-union and, over time, SNAC wrist arthritis.

Treatment

  • Undisplaced waist fractures: cast immobilisation or percutaneous headless screw fixation.
  • Displaced fractures and proximal pole fractures: screw fixation.
  • Non-union: bone graft with fixation; vascularised grafts for avascular fragments.
  • Salvage for established arthritis includes proximal row carpectomy or four-corner fusion.

Pitfalls

Screw too long or prominent into the joint, persistent stiffness and non-union. Smoking impairs healing.

Key points

  • Treat suspected scaphoid injuries as fractures until proven otherwise.
  • Proximal pole fractures heal poorly.
  • Untreated non-union leads to predictable arthritis.
