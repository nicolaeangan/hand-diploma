Carpal Tunnel Syndrome and Release

Median nerve compression: diagnosis, splinting and injection, and open versus endoscopic release.

RegionHand
TypeCondition
Updated2026-07-27
Reading time2 min
Connected20 articles

Overview

Carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS) is the commonest entrapment neuropathy. The median nerve is compressed beneath the transverse carpal ligament, causing numbness and tingling in the thumb, index, middle and radial half of the ring finger.

Diagnosis

  • Night-time paraesthesia, shaking the hand for relief, clumsiness.
  • Phalen, Tinel and carpal compression tests; thenar wasting in advanced disease.
  • Nerve conduction studies for atypical or severe cases and before surgery in some pathways.
  • Consider cervical radiculopathy, pronator syndrome and polyneuropathy.

Treatment

  • Neutral wrist night splints and corticosteroid injection for mild to moderate disease.
  • Carpal tunnel release for persistent symptoms or motor involvement.
  • Open or endoscopic release under local anaesthesia, usually as a day case.
  • Opponensplasty in rare cases of fixed loss of thumb opposition.

Pitfalls

Pillar pain and scar tenderness, incomplete release, recurrence and, rarely, injury to the palmar cutaneous or recurrent motor branch. Complex regional pain syndrome is uncommon but serious.

Key points

  • Symptom relief is excellent after release.
  • Wasting means recovery will be partial.
  • Question the diagnosis when symptoms persist.
